Two guards, deliberately different shapes
One is a decision, so it interrupts. The other is information, so it never does. Getting these the same way round is how a warning becomes wallpaper.

Un-complete — the cascade
Un-complete Wednesday’s session?
• Your block goes back to week 3. It’s on week 4 now.
• Next session’s targets are worked out again.
Keep it completed
Un-complete
Gated on the cascade existing. If un-completing moves nothing, there is no dialog at all — a modal that fires every time is a modal nobody reads. No type-to-confirm: that is reserved for breaking infrastructure, and using it here is exactly the overuse that trains people to click through.
Session un-completed · block back to week 3 Undo
And an undo after it. The dialog handles the moment; undo handles the year, when the dialog has become furniture.

Correcting a weight — the reach

•Your best bench set — 100 kg from 14 Aug is no longer your top lift.
•Next week’s bench target.
Your training max stays at 102.5 kg. It went up on 14 Aug and you’ve trained three sessions there since.
Shown only when a settled decision is affected. An ordinary typo fix gets nothing — put this on every edit and it becomes the thing people scroll past to reach the save button.
The training-max line is the whole reason this exists. Losing a personal record is obvious and expected. A number quietly not moving is the one nobody predicts, and the one that makes the app look wrong three weeks later.
Why they differ
A modal is a decision. A disclosure is information. Spend an interruption only where the user must choose.
