# Corpus slice 3: published research protocols and physical therapy / rehab

Classified against the round-2 algebra in `arena2/synthesis/` (algebra.ts, structure.ts, programs.ts, rationale.md).

## Bucket definitions used here

- **NATIVE**: expressible today. The constructs are named in the row.
- **ONE-NODE**: one addition to a registry (`Inputs`/`INPUT_DECLS`, `Scales`/`SCALE_LEVELS`, `BaseDim`/`Dim`/`UNITS`, `EventView.feedback`'s scale union, `AggQuery`) or exactly one new field/variant on an existing former. The language shape stays the same. The row names the addition.
- **MODEL CHANGE**: needs a new concept in the engine model, such as a new plan outcome, a new performed-data granularity, or a new kind of constraint. The row names it.
- **OUT OF SCOPE**: the row argues why.

Evidence column: **FT** = protocol text read in the full paper (pdftotext of the PDF); **AB** = abstract or index record plus secondary summaries; **REC** = from recall, so the specific numbers are not verified by this worker; **GL** = guideline or patient leaflet page.

## Patterns this slice demands

1. **A pain scale, read on three clocks.** Rehab progression and regression are driven by pain far more than by performance. The algebra has `jointPain: 0..3`, and only as event feedback. The protocols need a 0-10 scale (VAS in mm in Beyer, quantized), read **during** the session (`ev.feedback`), the **next morning** (a pre-session input that refers back to the previous session), and **week over week** (a state latch). ONE-NODE: `Scales.pain` + `SCALE_LEVELS` + a `pain` input + adding `'pain'` to `EventView.feedback`'s scale union. It unlocks Alfredson, Silbernagel, GLA:D and HSR's guard. Note the **inverted gate** (Alfredson: pain is expected, and progression waits for its absence) and the **ceiling gate** (Silbernagel/Beyer: ≤5/10 or 40-50 mm).
2. **Regression is ordinary, but rest is not.** Lowering load or sets on a flare is a native `commit`/`propose`. "Take 2 days off" (Fees soreness rules) and the 6-week active-rest arm (Silbernagel 2007) are not, because a plan must issue a session. MODEL CHANGE: a **rest/skip plan outcome**, issued as a fact like any prescription and described ("rest today: soreness persisted through the warm-up").
3. **Criteria gates over measured symmetry, with time floors.** ACL and rotator cuff phases advance on conjunctions of measurements (LSI ≥ 90% across 5 tests, effusion ≤ trace, ROM targets) AND a minimum time since surgery. Native today: time floors (`bounded.min` from a `startOn` anchor) and conjunctions (`logic`, which the phrase budget forces into named sub-criteria, the intended behaviour). Gaps: (a) measurements arrive as **inputs**, but `advanceWhen` grants only `HandoffCap='peer'`, so every criterion has to be latched into slot state by a `weekEnd` handler. That works but is indirect; granting `input` to `advanceWhen` is a one-line cap change worth considering. (b) **`bounded.max` auto-advances** whether or not the criteria hold. A safety gate must never time out into the next phase. ONE-NODE: `bounded.atMax: 'advance' | 'propose'` (propose = escalate to the owner or clinician, consistent with the forced-owner-confirm rule). (c) Macro phases only move forward, so regressing a whole phase is unrepresentable. Within-phase levels in state cover every protocol in this slice.
4. **Range of motion as a metric and as a ceiling.** `BaseDim` has no angle. ONE-NODE: `BaseDim 'angle'` + unit `deg` (for ROM inputs and criteria). Rotator cuff phase 2 also needs ROM as a **prescribed limit on a set** ("passive ER to at most N°"), which is a new set field: MODEL CHANGE. The limit rises over the weeks the way a load schedule does.
5. **Frequency within a day.** Alfredson and Tyler are twice a day or daily, the post-THR leaflet asks for 5 times a day plus hourly walks, and McKenzie is every 2 hours. The count can be encoded natively (a weekly `Rotation` with 7·n entries), but the prose is wrong ("14 days a week"). ONE-NODE: a `Rotation {k:'daily', perDay}` variant. Time-of-day spacing ("every 2 hours") is a reminder or scheduling concern and out of scope for program logic. Progression **per calendar day** needs `pos.day`: ONE-NODE.
6. **Caps that are not targets: RPE and HR as session constraints.** Cardiac rehab bounds every set by an HR ceiling (taken from a stress-test input) and a Borg 6-20 band, ends the session on symptoms, and progresses duration before intensity. Borg session RPE is not RIR and must not be unified with `effort`. HR is `{time:-1}` (the IR can represent it; the TS `Dim` table cannot). MODEL CHANGE: an aerobic intensity domain (HR / %HRR targets and ceilings) and **live session invariants** that can end a session.
7. **Load that is not a mass.** Elastic bar colour (Tyler), TheraBand grade (GLA:D) and isometric MVC force (Rio) all progress, but not as kilograms. Exercise ladders (`nth` over `List1<exercise>`) cover the elastic cases natively. Force would need its own dimension if MVC were ever more than "e1rm of the isometric".
8. **Set structure vs intensifier.** Cluster sets and BFR are properties of every set or of the whole session, not one-off intensifiers. The dose law correctly refuses `technique('cluster')` on every set, so both need their own field rather than a loosened dose law.
9. **Data within the set.** Velocity-loss termination (Pareja-Blanco) is the one research protocol that breaks the model: the rep count is an outcome of a drop inside the set measured against that set's own best rep. That needs performed data at rep level. Everything else in the research half fits, which says the algebra is calibrated for strength-research programming and stretched by clinical programming.
10. **Responding to symptoms within the session is diagnosis.** McKenzie's "peripheralized on this set → change direction now" is the clean OUT OF SCOPE case. Exercise choice stays a plan position, and the self-care subset can be expressed with a `symptomLocation` scale once a clinician has fixed the direction.
